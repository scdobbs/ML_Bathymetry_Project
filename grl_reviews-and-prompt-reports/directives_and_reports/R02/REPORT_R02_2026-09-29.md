## 1. Bottom line

1. **Challenge clause: no error found in §2.0–2.1.** One caveat recorded, not an error: the directive's theoretical
   receptive-field half-width (≈ 60 px) exceeds RIM 48; the *measured* effective field is r99 ≈ 47 px (§4.2), so 48 px
   is supported by measurement, marginally on NA080 and TN299 (r99 51–54 px).
2. **Leak test: PASS, 100/100 tiles** (§2.3). Inputs and `scale` byte-identical with the HR grid replaced by noise; the
   label changed on 100/100. The submitted pipeline's AUV normalization is gone from every input.
3. **§3.1a: the effective field is local and the border is tame.** 99% of gradient mass within 48 px (old model:
   r99 165 px); edge error 1.1–1.6× interior, within 20% of interior by 0–35 px on every unit (§4.3). Seam check:
   overlapping windows disagree on σ by 11–37% of the σ level on 7 pairs, 69% on NA080 (§4.4).
4. **μ barely beats the bicubic ship grid.** Median-of-units RMS 2.54 m vs 2.71 m (−6%); CNN better on 62% of tiles,
   median per-tile ratio 0.977; worse than bicubic on TN399, a tie on NA080 and FK181031. **In the fine band μ adds no
   power beyond the bicubic and is barely more coherent with HR** (median fine coherence 0.38 vs 0.34; band-power ratio
   to HR within ±0.1 of the bicubic's on 5 of 7 units). The model is the bicubic plus a small correction; there is no
   evidence of recovered sub-ship-resolution seafloor structure (§5.1–5.2).
5. **σ vs free baselines on T-ship (primary, mosaic): within-unit R² σ 0.799, roughness-3 0.749, free combined GBT
   0.763.** Δ(σ − free) = +0.036 [95% CI −0.042, +0.087], better on 5/7 units; Δ(σ − roughness-3) = +0.050
   [−0.016, +0.102]. Spearman ρ median is *lower* for σ (0.846 vs 0.874 free, 0.863 roughness). Top-20% capture
   0.755 vs 0.730 vs 0.743. On T-cnn: Δ(σ − free) = +0.043 [−0.035, +0.096]. **No CI excludes zero against the
   roughness or free-combined baselines; σ clearly beats only the metadata baselines.** No winner designated.
6. **Incremental value of σ over free features: Δ(σ + free − free) = +0.021 [−0.017, +0.049]** on T-ship,
   +0.027 [−0.018, +0.068] on T-cnn. Not distinguishable from zero at N = 7 units.
7. **The paper's headline gap (σ 0.555 vs roughness-3 0.107) does not survive the clean pipeline** (§6): with roughness
   recomputed in meters on ship data alone, roughness-3 scores 0.749, not 0.107. The submitted gap was mostly the

## 4. §3.1 — Inference and the §3.1a re-measurement

### 4.1 What went wrong in the first mosaic job, and the fix (CONFIRMED)

`extract_clean_patches.tile_ship_side` hands the *entire* LR raster to `rasterio.warp.reproject` for every window;
the cost is 5 ms on NA090 (79 × 79 LR) but 50–315 ms on NA080/TN399/RR1506 (up to 613 × 369), run-to-run variable.
NA080 (7,488 windows) alone took 52 min; TN399 did not finish. A cropped-source variant is 40–60× faster but was
**rejected**: on 1 of 126 test tiles it changed one LR cell's validity (a destination cell centre falling on a source
pixel boundary after the transform is re-composed; not fixed by any margin), so it is not bit-identical to the stored
patches. The fix used instead keeps the exact function and moves it off the critical path: `precompute_lr_tiles.py`
(CPU, 16 workers, job 45897637, 12 min) caches `tile_ship_side` output for all 34,875 mosaic windows plus the seam
windows; `mosaic_infer.py` reads the cache (`--cache_dir`) and falls back to the original call on a miss. A CPU
end-to-end test on NA090 (job 45897691) reproduced the GPU run's `covered`, `joint` and `lr_up_m` arrays bit-for-bit;
μ and σ differ between CPU and GPU bf16 autocast by RMS 0.006 m and 0.014 m (σ level 0.73 m). All nine pairs were then
mosaicked on CPU in one job (45899807) for uniformity; the two GPU mosaics are kept in `mosaics_gpu_partial/`.
Original script: `profile/mosaic_infer.py.bak_45797098`.
